A rule is `confirmed by oracle` when those cases exercise it as defined in this file; the line `Oracle:` names them. That tag speaks about the definitions and the arithmetic, not about the reference software: whether the reference software uses the same definitions, and its defaults, stay open items (section 12) and the defaults of `AGENTS.md` section 6 stay `assumed`. A statistical formula that is standard and found in an allowed source but not exercised is `documented, untested`. The defaults that `AGENTS.md` section 6 records as seen on the human's screen (increment 0.001, convergence 0.0001, 50 iterations, 1000 predicted points, uniform weighting, generated bounds, Gauss-Newton with the Levenberg and Hartley modification) are `assumed` until a private oracle confirms them: the reader may not read the reference software's documentation and found no open source that states them. Design choices of Caladrius are `assumed`.

**Rule ids.** `FIT-<AREA>-<nn>`. Areas: OBJ (objective), WGT (weights), ALG (algorithm), JAC (derivatives), CNV (convergence), BND (bounds), INI (initial estimates), OUT (outputs), FLG (quality flags), ERR (errors), VOC (vocabulary).

**Clean room.** Textbooks cannot be read by the reader (S-28 is cited at book level). The method rests on the original papers of Levenberg, Hartley and Marquardt (S-23, S-24, S-25; bibliographic records checked, papers not read), on the conventions of R's `nls` (S-26), and on the standard statistics of non-linear regression, derived here (D). Where the reference software's own details are unknown they are named as open items (section 12), never guessed silently.

**Two optimizers do not reach the same minimum bit for bit** (`AGENTS.md` section 5). A gap beyond the fit tolerances is a method difference to be recorded in `specs/differences.md`, not a reason to widen a tolerance.

## 1. Notation

| symbol | meaning |
|---|---|
| (t_i, y_i), i = 1 … N | observations used in the fit (after exclusions, FIT-ERR-01) |
| θ = (θ_1 … θ_P) | model parameters being estimated; P of them (fixed parameters are not counted) |
| ŷ_i = f(t_i; θ) | model prediction (`specs/models.md`) |
| w_i | weight of observation i (FIT-WGT-01) |
| r_i | residual y_i − ŷ_i; weighted residual √w_i·r_i |
| WRSS(θ) | weighted residual sum of squares Σ w_i·r_i² (the objective) |
| J | weighted Jacobian, J_ij = √w_i·∂f(t_i; θ)/∂θ_j (N × P) |
| DF | degrees of freedom N − P |
| S² = WRSS/DF, S | residual variance and its square root (the standard error of the fit) |
| Cov | variance–covariance matrix of the estimates, S²·(JᵀJ)⁻¹ |

## 2. Objective and weights

### FIT-OBJ-01 Objective
Minimise WRSS(θ) = Σ w_i·(y_i − f(t_i; θ))² over the free parameters, subject to bounds (FIT-BND-01). The estimate is a minimiser; local minima are possible (non-linear model), so the result is "a" minimum reached from the initial estimates.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the minimiser of every one of the 90 reference fits (estimates 1e-4 relative, WRSS 1e-6 relative), E-10.
- Sources: S-28 (weighted non-linear least squares, book level); S-23, S-24, S-25; D.

### FIT-WGT-01 Weighting schemes
Five schemes, selectable by name:

| id | weight w_i | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `uniform` | 1 | ordinary least squares |
| `inv_y` | 1/y_i | variance proportional to the observed value |
| `inv_y2` | 1/y_i² | constant coefficient of variation, on the observed values |
| `inv_yhat` | 1/ŷ_i | as `inv_y`, on the predicted values |
| `inv_yhat2` | 1/ŷ_i² | as `inv_y2`, on the predicted values |

Uniform weighting on the observed values is the default (`AGENTS.md` section 6). The weights do not depend on the parameters for the first three schemes; for the last two they do (FIT-WGT-03). Weights are used as given and are not rescaled; the estimates and the covariance matrix are unchanged if all weights are multiplied by the same positive constant, but WRSS, S² and the information criteria change (property used as a test, example F2).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: all five weightings on `fit_theoph_*` (12 subjects each), `fit_indometh_*` (6 subjects each) and `fit_spec_*` (E-10). The default (uniform) and the non-rescaling of weights are pinned by the defaults test; that uniform is the reference software's default is the human's observation, `assumed`.
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` sections 4 and 6 (the five schemes and the default); S-28 (weighted least squares, book level); D (invariance: Cov = S²·(JᵀJ)⁻¹, with S² and (JᵀJ)⁻¹ scaling by c and 1/c).

### FIT-WGT-02 Observed values that cannot be weighted
With `inv_y` or `inv_y2` an observation with y ≤ 0, or so small that its weight is not finite (below about 1e-154 for `inv_y2`), has an undefined or infinite weight. The run stops before fitting with an error (`UnweightableObservation`: "zero, negative, or too small to weight") that names the observations (time and value) and offers two ways out: exclude them (they stay in the table with the reason `zero_weight` and are not counted in N), or choose another scheme. Nothing is replaced by a tiny number silently.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the test "observed-value weights refuse y ≤ 0" of `oracle_fit.rs` (E-10); the too-small case by an engine unit test (E-11).
- Sources: golden rule 6 of `AGENTS.md` (zero or negative concentrations give a readable error); design choice.

### FIT-WGT-03 Predicted-value weights
For `inv_yhat` and `inv_yhat2` the weights are recomputed from the current predictions at the start of every iteration and are treated as constants when the derivatives are formed (iteratively reweighted least squares); the final statistics use the weights at the solution. The target of such a fit is therefore a fixed point θ with Jᵀ·W(θ)·r(θ) = 0, W from the predictions at θ, which is not the minimiser of Σ r²/ŷ² with the weight differentiated too (open item OF-04, untested by design). A trial point with a prediction ŷ_i ≤ 0 is rejected like a step that does not decrease WRSS (FIT-ALG-03). An observation whose prediction is exactly 0 at every parameter value (an oral model at t = 0 or before the lag) cannot carry a predicted-value weight: the caller leaves it out, as the oracle does for the Theoph fits (their sample at t = 0 is not used).

What the oracle established about these fits (E-10; the first version of the Indometh cases was wrong and was corrected by card T-018):
- On Theoph all five weightings settle by the plain "refit with frozen weights, then reweight" iteration.
- On Indometh (a one-compartment model fitted to a biexponential profile, a poor but legitimate fit) the plain iteration cycles between two estimates for `inv_yhat2` on 5 of the 6 subjects and does not settle for `inv_yhat`. The fixed points exist nevertheless, one per subject and scheme over a grid of starts (V from 1 to 300, k from 0.02 to 10), found by root finding on the stationarity equations and confirmed in 200-bit arithmetic. `stats::nls` fails to converge for 5 of the 6 `inv_yhat2` subjects, which is why the references come from root finding there.
- Points that look like solutions but are not: the first oracle accepted, for `inv_yhat` subjects 1, 3, 5 and 6, estimates with V near 0.004, k near 28 and WRSS near 1e89, because its stationarity test was divided by a quantity containing the weights, which explodes there. An acceptance test must be scale-free (FIT-WGT-04).
- The engine reaches the genuine fixed points by damped steps (FIT-ALG-04, FIT-CNV-05).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: `fit_theoph_inv_yhat`, `fit_theoph_inv_yhat2` (12 subjects, solved by iteration), `fit_indometh_inv_yhat` (6 subjects, all by root finding), `fit_indometh_inv_yhat2` (subject 2 by iteration, the other five by root finding), `fit_spec_inv_yhat`, `fit_spec_inv_yhat2`; the engine matches all of them at the 1e-4 and 1e-6 tolerances (E-10, E-11). Worked example F5.
- Sources: S-28 (iterative reweighting, book level); design choice; E-10, E-11 (cards T-009, T-018, T-011a).

### FIT-WGT-04 How a predicted-weight solution is verified
A candidate (V, k, …) is a fixed point when the stationarity sums Σ_i w_i·r_i·∂f_i/∂θ_j (weights at the candidate) cancel: the sum of each parameter's terms, divided by the sum of the absolute values of those terms, is at rounding level. The oracle accepts a root at 1e-10 and a settled iteration at 1e-6. The normaliser must not contain the weights or the predictions, because those can be astronomically large at spurious points. Used as a test property for the engine (independent evaluation of Jᵀ·W·r).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the 12 Indometh subject–scheme pairs of `fit_indometh_inv_yhat` and `fit_indometh_inv_yhat2`; worked example F5 gives the cancellation measure at two of them (E-10).
- Sources: D; card T-018 (E-10).

## 3. Algorithm

### FIT-ALG-01 Default algorithm
The default minimiser is the Gauss-Newton method with the Levenberg and Hartley modification: a Gauss-Newton direction, a step-length reduction along it (Hartley), and a damping term added to the normal equations when the plain direction fails or the system is ill-conditioned (Levenberg). Another optimizer may be offered as an option, never as a silent replacement (`AGENTS.md` section 6). The exact internals of the reference software are not known; FIT-ALG-02 to FIT-ALG-05 specify what Caladrius does.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6 (the method named as seen on the human's screen); S-24 (Levenberg: damped least squares), S-23 (Hartley: modified Gauss-Newton), S-25 (Marquardt: the scaled form).

### FIT-ALG-02 Gauss-Newton direction
At θ_k with weighted residual vector r = (√w_i·r_i) and weighted Jacobian J, the direction δ solves the normal equations (JᵀJ + λ·M)·δ = Jᵀr, with λ = 0 for the pure Gauss-Newton direction. M is the identity for Levenberg's original damping and diag(JᵀJ) for Marquardt's scale-invariant form; Caladrius uses diag(JᵀJ) (parameters such as V ≈ 10 and k ≈ 0.2 differ by orders of magnitude). Numerically the system is solved from J, with its columns scaled to equal length, by a QR (Householder) factorisation, not by forming JᵀJ explicitly, because JᵀJ squares the condition number. A pure Gauss-Newton step needs P ≤ N and a non-singular JᵀJ.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the iterates of worked example F1 (`oracle/expected/fit/gauss_newton_f1.csv`, WRSS and (V, k) for iterations 0 to 5 at 15 digits, compared at 1e-9 relative): the pure Gauss-Newton step with ν = 1 is accepted each time (E-10, E-11).
- Sources: S-24, S-25 (the damped normal equations); S-28 (Gauss-Newton, book level); D (setting the gradient of WRSS to zero and dropping the second-derivative term). Worked example F1.

### FIT-ALG-03 Step length (Hartley)
Given δ, try θ_k + ν·δ with ν = 1; while WRSS(θ_k + ν·δ) ≥ WRSS(θ_k) or the point is outside the bounds or the prediction is not finite, halve ν, down to ν_min = 2⁻¹⁰ (the default floor of R's `nls`, S-26). A step that decreases WRSS is accepted and the iteration counter advances.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: S-23 (the modification of Gauss-Newton by a step length); S-26 (step halving with a floor); design choice for the numbers.

### FIT-ALG-04 Damping (Levenberg)
If no ν ≥ ν_min decreases WRSS, or the factorisation reports a numerically singular system, repeat FIT-ALG-02 with λ > 0: start at λ = 1e-3, multiply by 10 after each failure up to 1e10; after a success divide λ by 10 (never below 0, returning to the pure Gauss-Newton direction). If λ = 1e10 still fails the run stops with status `no_decrease` (FIT-CNV-03).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: S-24, S-25 (increase the damping until the sum of squares falls); design choice for the factors.

### FIT-ALG-05 Determinism
The same input, options and initial estimates always give the same output and trace, on every platform (no randomisation, no threads that change summation order).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the determinism test of `oracle_fit.rs`: the same input gives identical output (E-10).
- Sources: golden rule 3 and 4 of `AGENTS.md` (the same command from UI, CLI and MCP); design choice.

## 4. Partial derivatives

### FIT-JAC-01 Finite differences (default)
The default partial derivative of the prediction with respect to θ_j is the forward difference [f(θ + Δ_j·e_j) − f(θ)]/Δ_j with Δ_j = h·|θ_j|, h = 0.001 (a relative increment), and Δ_j = h when θ_j = 0. The increment `h` is a user option.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6 ("increment for partial derivatives 0.001"); the relative and forward nature of the increment is the reader's reading of it (open item OF-01). Worked example F2 shows the size of the effect; the forward-difference standard errors are reproduced by the oracle test of FIT-JAC-03. The value 0.001 and its reading are the human's observation and stay `assumed`.

### FIT-JAC-02 Analytic derivatives (option)
The models of `specs/models.md` have closed-form derivatives. They may be used instead (`derivatives = analytic`), and the testkit uses them as an independent truth. For the IV bolus, ∂f/∂V = −f/V and ∂f/∂k = −t·f (in the (V, k) parameterisation). The engine has closed-form derivatives for `pk1.iv_bolus` ((V, k) and (V, CL)) and `pk1.oral_1` ((V, k, ka), with a series near ka = k); for every other model `analytic` is refused with an error (`AnalyticDerivativesUnavailable`) that says to use forward differences; it does not fall back silently (this sentence said the opposite until T-029, which found it by running the new fit oracles). The oracle's references use analytic derivatives.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: all 90 reference fits are computed on the analytic Jacobian; the engine's analytic bolus and first-order derivatives reproduce them (E-10, E-11).
- Sources: D (calculus). Worked examples F1, F2.

### FIT-JAC-03 Which Jacobian the statistics use
The standard errors and the matrices of FIT-OUT-02 use the same Jacobian as the last iteration of the fit (finite differences by default). Their size therefore carries the relative error of the increment (about h/2 times the curvature); with h = 0.001 this is of order 1e-3 relative on the standard errors (example F2).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the test "forward-difference SE of F2": with h = 0.001 the standard errors of the five-point example are 0.24814908 and 0.01281461 (E-10).
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6; D; H3.

## 5. Convergence and non-convergence

### FIT-CNV-01 Convergence criterion
Let WRSS_k be the value after accepted iteration k (WRSS_0 at the initial estimates). The fit has converged at iteration k when the relative decrease is small: (WRSS_{k−1} − WRSS_k) ≤ ε·WRSS_k, with ε = 0.0001 (default). The result is θ_k. The engine adds two ways to end at a minimum that is as good as rounding allows: (a) after at least one accepted step, no trial step decreases WRSS and the decrease predicted by the Gauss-Newton model (‖J·δ‖²) is at most ε·WRSS; (b) an exact fit, WRSS ≤ 1e-26·Σ w·y². Neither is taken at the initial estimates: a start from which nothing can be improved is `no_decrease`, not `converged`. For predicted-value weights the test of FIT-CNV-05 replaces the plain one. Alternative criterion `relative_offset` (the one of R's `nls`: the size of the residual's component along the model's tangent plane relative to the part orthogonal to it, over the free parameters) is an option; it is scale-free and is the better test near a perfect fit.
- Status: `assumed`
- Oracle: the iteration count of F1: stop at k = 4 with the default ε (unit test of the engine, and the trace of `gauss_newton_f1.csv`); the oracle itself runs with ε = 1e-10 (FIT-CNV-04). The value 0.0001 and the quantity it applies to are the open item OF-02.
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6 ("convergence criterion 0.0001": which quantity it applies to is not stated, open item OF-02); S-26 (relative offset criterion, default 1e-5); D. Worked example F1 shows the stop after 4 iterations.

### FIT-CNV-02 Maximum number of iterations
At most 50 accepted iterations (default). Reaching 50 without meeting FIT-CNV-01 is not convergence (status `max_iterations`; the iteration-limit test of `oracle_fit.rs` checks it).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6; S-26 (default 50).

### FIT-CNV-05 Convergence with predicted-value weights
Because the weights are frozen inside an iteration (FIT-WGT-03), the decrease of WRSS measured with them understates how far the reweighting still has to go. With predicted-value weights the engine therefore estimates the linear rate of the iteration from the last two relative decreases, ρ = √(r_k / r_{k−1}), and declares convergence only when ρ < 1 and r_k ≤ ε·(1 − ρ)², that is when the expected remaining gain of the reweighting is below the criterion. This is stricter than the plain test (it reduces to it when ρ → 0), cannot fire at the first iteration, and never fires on a cycle (ρ ≥ 1) or on growing decreases. Before `converged` or `at_bound` is reported, the WRSS of the final estimates, computed with the weights of those same estimates (its own weights), is compared with that of the initial estimates; if it is larger, the status is `no_decrease` and the iterate with the lowest finite own-weight WRSS is returned, so a fit is never called converged when it ended worse than it began.
- Status: `assumed`
- Oracle: needed to reach the Indometh `inv_yhat2` subject 4 value at 1e-6 relative in WRSS: the plain test stopped at WRSS 2.0117000238 against the fixed point 2.0116979316 (1.04e-6 relative), because WRSS is not stationary at a reweighted fixed point (d ln WRSS / d ln V is 1 for `inv_yhat` and 2 for `inv_yhat2`), so an estimate error of 3e-7 appears as 1e-6 in WRSS (E-10, E-11). The orchestrator decided that the engine converges tighter rather than the tolerance being changed.
- Sources: design choice of card T-011a, reviewed (E-11).

### FIT-CNV-03 Status and reasons
Every fit ends with a status, never with a panic or a silent value:

| status | meaning | what is returned |
|---|---|---|
| `converged` | FIT-CNV-01 met | everything in section 7 |
| `max_iterations` | 50 iterations done, criterion not met | best iterate, trace, statistics flagged "not converged" |
| `no_decrease` | FIT-ALG-03/04 cannot decrease WRSS, or (predicted-value weights) the final own-weight WRSS is above that of the initial estimates (FIT-CNV-05) | best iterate, trace, statistics flagged |
| `singular` | JᵀJ has a zero eigenvalue at the best iterate: at least one parameter combination is not identifiable | best iterate; standard errors NC (`singular`); the combination (eigenvector of the zero eigenvalue, named by parameter) is reported |
| `non_finite` | the model gave a non-finite value at the start or at every trial | error before or at the first iteration, naming the parameters |
| `at_bound` | converged with at least one parameter on a bound | converged result, with the parameters on a bound listed (FIT-BND-02) |

Input that cannot be weighted, an unusable initial estimate or an unusable option is an error before the fit starts (FIT-ERR-01, FIT-ERR-04), not a status.

The text of every status says what to change (initial estimates, bounds, weights, model). The statistics of a result that did not converge are shown only with a visible "not converged" mark.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: golden rule 6 of `AGENTS.md` (non-convergence and singular matrix each give a readable error and a regression test); `AGENTS.md` section 7 (every error says what to fix).

### FIT-CNV-04 Conformance runs
Tests compare minima with a tight criterion (ε = 1e-10 on the relative decrease, 200 iterations) so that parameters are compared at the tolerances of `AGENTS.md` section 5 (1e-4 relative; WRSS 1e-6 relative); the user-facing default stays ε = 1e-4. With ε = 1e-4 the first example stops at parameters that are already within 1e-7 relative of the minimum (F1), but this is not guaranteed in general.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: every reference fit and `gauss_newton_f1.csv` use convergence 1e-10 and 200 iterations; the engine's results are compared at those settings (E-10).
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 5; D; H3.

## 6. Bounds and initial estimates

### FIT-BND-01 Bounds
Each parameter has an optional lower and upper bound (user supplied, default FIT-BND-03). The initial estimate must lie inside; otherwise an error names the parameter. A trial point outside the bounds is shortened (FIT-ALG-03); if the Gauss-Newton direction for a parameter that sits on a bound points outward, that parameter is held at the bound for the iteration and the direction is recomputed for the remaining free parameters (active-set projection).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6 ("parameter bounds generated by the software unless the user supplies them"); design choice for the projection.

### FIT-BND-02 Reporting a bound
A parameter held on a bound at the end gets the flag `at_bound`; it gets no standard error or interval; the other parameters' statistics are computed from the free-parameter block only (P and DF count the free parameters) and are conditional on the bound. A result with a parameter on a bound is never presented as an ordinary converged fit.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: design choice; `AGENTS.md` section 7 (poor fits must be flagged).

### FIT-BND-03 Default bounds
When the user supplies none, the engine generates bounds from the initial estimates: `v`, `cl`, `k`, `ka` and `dur` must stay above 1e-6 × their initial estimate, `tlag` ≥ 0, and there is no upper bound. An initial estimate outside its bounds is an error (`InitialOutsideBounds`). What the reference software generates is not known (open item OF-03). Parameters can also be held fixed (option `fixed`, for example `dur` of a zero-order model, which cannot be estimated from initial values alone); a fixed parameter given an initial estimate or a bound is refused.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6; `specs/models.md` MOD-GEN-04 (parameter domains); engine rule of card T-011b (E-11).

### FIT-INI-01 Initial estimates
User-supplied initial estimates are used as given. When absent, they are generated by the method of residuals (FIT-INI-02 to 04). Generated values are shown to the user and can be edited; the predicted curve updates live (`AGENTS.md` section 7).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` sections 6 and 7; S-28 (curve stripping, book level).

### FIT-INI-02 IV bolus
Fit the straight line ln y = a − k·t by ordinary least squares on all points with y > 0 (all points belong to the single phase of a one-compartment model; the last three or more if the user restricts it). Then k = −slope and V = D/e^a. Points with y ≤ 0 are skipped for this step only.
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: S-28 (log-linear regression for the one-compartment bolus, book level); `specs/nca.md` NCA-LZ-01 (the same regression); D. Worked example F4 uses the same machinery.

### FIT-INI-03 First-order absorption (curve stripping)
1. Terminal phase: choose the terminal points by the rule of `specs/nca.md` NCA-LZ-02 to NCA-LZ-05 (after the observed Tmax, y > 0, best adjusted R² within the tolerance, at least 3 points). Its line is ln y = a1 − k·t.
2. Residuals: for each point before the terminal window with a positive residual R(t) = e^(a1 − k·t) − y(t), the residuals follow A·e^(−ka·t) in a one-compartment model. Fit ln R = a2 − ka·t by ordinary least squares (at least 2 points; the points with R ≤ 0 are skipped).
3. Constants: A = e^(a1) for the model without lag. V/F = D·ka/((ka − k)·A).
4. Lag: for the model with lag, the two lines meet at tlag (D: A·e^(−k(t − tlag)) and A·e^(−ka(t − tlag)) are both equal to A at t = tlag), so tlag = (a1 − a2)/(k − ka), set to 0 if negative; and A = e^(a1 − k·tlag).
5. Flip-flop: if the residual slope ka comes out smaller than k, the roles are ambiguous (`specs/models.md` MOD-AB1-06); the default assigns the faster constant to ka and warns that the other solution exists.

If fewer than 3 terminal points or fewer than 2 positive residuals are available, no automatic estimate is produced: an error asks the user to enter estimates (FIT-ERR-03). The generated lag is kept below 0.9 times the first sampling time, so that no prediction is zero at the first sample. The engine reproduces example F4 (k 0.19968599, ka 1.0370213, V 9.9716735, tlag 0.0347); it resolves the flip-flop towards the faster constant silently, no warning channel exists yet (open item OF-11). Example F4 gives the numbers.
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: S-28 (method of residuals, book level); `specs/nca.md` NCA-LZ-02..05 (point selection); D. The lag-from-intersection step and the flip-flop warning are the reader's, tagged with the rule.

### FIT-INI-04 Infusion and zero-order input
Terminal phase after the end of the input (time T + tlag) as in FIT-INI-03 step 1, line ln y = a1 − k·t. The concentration at the end of the input is C_T = e^(a1 − k·(T + tlag)). With R0 = D/T: CL = R0·(1 − e^(−k·T))/C_T and V = CL/k (D: from MOD-IVI-01). If no point lies after the end of the input, only the rising phase is available and no automatic estimate is produced (FIT-ERR-03). The duration is not estimated from the data: it must be given as a fixed parameter (finite and > 0, otherwise the error names it).
- Status: `documented, untested`
- Sources: `specs/models.md` MOD-IVI-01; S-28; D.

## 7. Outputs

The result of a fit contains the items below; each is NC with a reason when it cannot be computed (never NaN or infinity). Items 1 to 5 are the "final parameters" table.

### FIT-OUT-01 Final parameters
For each free parameter: the estimate, its standard error, CV%, univariate confidence interval (lower, upper), planar confidence interval (lower, upper). Fixed parameters are listed with their value and no statistics.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: estimate, SE, CV%, both intervals for every parameter of the 90 fits (E-10).
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 4 (list of outputs of `caladrius-fit`); the definitions follow.

### FIT-OUT-02 Covariance, standard errors, CV%
Cov = S²·(JᵀJ)⁻¹ with J the weighted Jacobian at the solution and S² = WRSS/DF. SE_j = √Cov_jj. CV%_j = 100·SE_j/|θ̂_j| (NC if θ̂_j = 0). When DF = 0 or JᵀJ is singular, these are NC (`no_degrees_of_freedom`, `singular`).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: covariance, correlation, SE and CV% of the 90 fits; SE and S checked against `summary.nls` to 1e-6 in the oracle script (E-10).
- Sources: S-28 (asymptotic covariance of non-linear least squares, book level); D (linearisation of the model at the solution); S-26 (the usual `summary` of a non-linear fit is of this form). Worked example F2.

### FIT-OUT-03 Univariate confidence interval
θ̂_j ± t(1 − α/2; DF)·SE_j, with the Student quantile for DF degrees of freedom and α = 0.05 by default (level 95 %). Each interval is for one parameter taken alone.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: univariate limits of the 90 fits at level 95 % (`confidence_level` 0.95, Student quantile), compared at the derived tolerance for limits near zero explained in the test header (E-10). Whether the reference software uses this definition is OF-05.
- Sources: S-28 (book level); D. That the reference software uses this definition and the level 95 % is not known (OF-05). Worked example F2.

### FIT-OUT-04 Planar confidence interval
The joint (tangent-plane) confidence region of all parameters is the ellipsoid (θ − θ̂)ᵀ·(JᵀJ)·(θ − θ̂) ≤ P·S²·F(1 − α; P, DF). The planar interval of parameter j is the extent of this ellipsoid along axis j: θ̂_j ± √(P·F(1 − α; P, DF))·SE_j. It is wider than the univariate interval because it covers all parameters at once. If the reference software's "planar" interval is another construction this is a method difference (OF-05).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: planar limits of the 90 fits (factor √(P·F(0.95; P, DF))), E-10. Whether the reference software's "planar" interval is this one is OF-05.
- Sources: S-28 (linear-approximation joint regions, book level); D (the extent of {x : xᵀAx ≤ c} along axis j is √(c·(A⁻¹)_jj)). Worked example F2.

### FIT-OUT-05 Correlation matrix, eigenvalues, condition number
Corr_jk = Cov_jk/(SE_j·SE_k) (symmetric, unit diagonal). Eigenvalues of the correlation matrix, in decreasing order. Condition number κ = largest/smallest eigenvalue of the correlation matrix (infinite when singular: NC). The condition number of the scaled Jacobian (columns of J normalised to unit length), √(λmax/λmin) of the scaled JᵀJ, is reported as `kappa_jacobian`. For two parameters the two sets of eigenvalues coincide (example F2).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: eigenvalues and condition number of the correlation matrix and the condition of the unit-column Jacobian, 90 fits (E-10); which matrix the reference software uses is OF-06.
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 4 (eigenvalues and condition numbers listed without a definition); D. Which matrix the reference software uses is OF-06.

### FIT-OUT-06 Secondary parameters
For a derived quantity g(θ) (CL = V·k, t½ = ln 2/k, AUC to infinity, Cmax, Tmax, …) the value g(θ̂) and the standard error √(∇gᵀ·Cov·∇g) (the delta method, gradient by differentiation of the model formulas or by the finite-difference increment), with CV% and the univariate interval as in FIT-OUT-02/03 using the same DF.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: CL, half-life and AUC to infinity with SE, CV% and interval by the delta method, 90 fits (E-10). The engine provides them through the model (`FitModel::secondary`); a custom model has none.
- Sources: S-28 (delta method, book level); `specs/models.md` MOD-SEC-02; D. Worked example F2 (CL and t½).

### FIT-OUT-07 Diagnostics
- weighted sum of squares of the observations: Σ w_i·y_i²;
- corrected weighted sum of squares: Σ w_i·(y_i − ȳ_w)² with ȳ_w = Σ w_i·y_i/Σ w_i (the weighted total variation around the weighted mean);
- weighted residual sum of squares WRSS (the objective), also called the residual SS;
- S = √(WRSS/DF), DF = N − P (NC for DF ≤ 0);
- correlation between observed and predicted: the weighted Pearson correlation of y and ŷ with weights w_i;
- AIC = N·ln(WRSS) + 2·P; SBC = N·ln(WRSS) + P·ln N.

The information criteria use this form (common in PK software and giving the same ranking as the likelihood form for the same data and weights); a constant N·(1 + ln(2π/N))-type term separates it from the likelihood form and from other programs' values (OF-07). NC if WRSS = 0 or the fit is exact (WRSS ≤ 1e-26·Σ w·y², where the logarithm is meaningless).
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: Σ w·y², corrected sum, WRSS, S, observed/predicted correlation, AIC and SBC of the 90 fits; AIC and SBC are compared through the derived tolerance of the test header (E-10). Whether the reference software prints the same forms is OF-07.
- Sources: S-27 (Akaike's criterion), S-29 (Schwarz's criterion), both as principles; the exact forms and the use of the weights are the reader's reading (OF-07); D. Worked example F2.

### FIT-OUT-08 Partial derivatives
A table with one row per observation and one column per parameter: the (unweighted) derivative ∂f(t_i)/∂θ_j at the solution, by the method of FIT-JAC-01 (or analytic). The weighted version √w_i·∂f/∂θ_j is available (empty when a final weight is not finite). The engine's table agrees with the analytic values of example F2 (its own unit test); the oracle has no partials file.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 4; D. Worked example F2 prints the table.

### FIT-OUT-09 Predicted data and residuals
For each observation: time, observed, predicted, residual y − ŷ, weighted residual √w·(y − ŷ), weight. Residuals of excluded observations are shown with their exclusion reason and are not used in any sum.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 4; design choice.

### FIT-OUT-10 Smooth predicted curve
`n_curve` = 1000 equally spaced times from the first to the last observation time (both included) with the predicted concentration, for plotting. The count is an option.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 6 ("1000 predicted values for the curve").

### FIT-OUT-11 Minimization trace
One row per iteration, including iteration 0: iteration number, WRSS, the parameter vector, the step factor ν actually used, λ, the relative decrease of FIT-CNV-01, and the number of step halvings. The last row carries the status of FIT-CNV-03 and the reason for stopping. Never discarded, also for failed fits. With predicted-value weights the last row may show an iterate that was not returned (FIT-CNV-05): the trace is a record, the reported estimates are the best iterate.
- Status: `assumed`
- Oracle: the iterates of F1 (iteration, WRSS, V, k) at 15 digits (E-10); the step factor, λ and halving columns are not covered.
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 4 (minimization trace); design choice. Worked example F1 prints a trace.

## 8. Quality flags and errors

### FIT-FLG-01 Flags
A poor fit is flagged, not hidden (`AGENTS.md` section 7, friction 4). The engine raises these flags, from the finished numbers, never feeding back into them:

| flag | raised when | default threshold |
|---|---|---|
| not converged | the status is not `converged` (or `at_bound`) | none |
| weak convergence | the last accepted step was damped (λ > 0) or shortened to ν ≤ 0.25, so that "converged" rests on a small decrease after a difficult step (for example a 3-point fit of a 3-parameter model); ordinary halvings are not flagged | none |
| at bound | a parameter ended on a bound | none |
| high CV | CV% of a parameter above the threshold | 50 |
| interval contains 0 | the univariate interval of a parameter that must be positive contains 0 | none |
| high correlation | \|correlation\| of two estimates above the threshold | 0.95 |
| ill-conditioned | condition number above the threshold | 1e6 |
| few degrees of freedom | DF below the threshold | 2 |

Thresholds are options, range-checked (CV% ≥ 0, correlation threshold in [0, 1], condition number ≥ 1, otherwise `InvalidOption`; a NaN threshold is refused). Each flag says what to check (fewer parameters, other initial estimates, a different weighting).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: `AGENTS.md` section 7; design choice (thresholds, wording); implemented in card T-011b and reviewed (E-11).

### FIT-ERR-01 Input errors (before fitting)
Readable errors for: observation arrays of different length; fewer observations than free parameters (N < P); NaN or infinite time or concentration (a missing concentration is excluded with the reason `missing`); negative times for IV models; negative concentrations when the weights need positive values (FIT-WGT-02); an initial estimate outside its bounds; a dose ≤ 0; an unknown model or weighting id. In the engine, following the oracle test, a NaN concentration and a time before the dose are errors, not silent exclusions: the caller leaves such observations out (the data layer may offer the exclusion with the reasons `missing` and `before_dose`).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: golden rule 6 of `AGENTS.md`; `specs/nca.md` NCA-DAT-02, NCA-DAT-03; design choice.

### FIT-ERR-02 N = P and N − P small
With N = P (DF = 0) the fit can still run and reaches WRSS ≈ 0 when it converges; S, standard errors, intervals and AIC are NC (`no_degrees_of_freedom`) and the result is flagged "exact fit, no precision information". The same holds for an exact fit with DF > 0 (WRSS ≤ 1e-26·Σ w·y²): AIC and SBC are NC there, since ln WRSS is meaningless. With DF ≥ 1 everything is computed, and DF < 2 is flagged.
- Status: `confirmed by oracle`
- Oracle: the N = P test of `oracle_fit.rs` (the statistics are absent, not NaN), E-10.
- Sources: D (S² = WRSS/DF is undefined for DF = 0); design choice.

### FIT-ERR-03 Initial estimates cannot be generated
Error text: the terminal phase has fewer than 3 usable points, or there are no earlier points to form residuals, or the residual line rises; the user is asked for estimates, with the data reduced to what was usable shown beside it.
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: golden rule 6 and `AGENTS.md` section 7; design choice.

### FIT-ERR-04 Options are range-checked
Options that could exhaust memory or make the run meaningless are refused before anything is allocated, with `InvalidOption` naming the option: `n_curve` must be 0 or between 2 and 1 000 000, `convergence` between 0 and 0.1, `max_iterations` at most 100 000, `increment` in (0, 0.1], the flag thresholds as in FIT-FLG-01. Unknown option names are errors too (a misspelled option never runs silently with the default).
- Status: `assumed`
- Sources: reviewer findings on card T-011a (an `n_curve` of 2⁴⁰ aborted the process), golden rule 6; design choice (E-11).

## 10. Why the defaults are credible: a note on convergence

(No rule.) Near a minimum with small residuals the Gauss-Newton iteration converges quadratically, so when the relative decrease of WRSS has fallen to 1e-4 the parameters are usually already much closer to the minimum than 1e-4 (example F1: 1e-7). With large residuals the convergence is linear and the same stopping rule can stop far from the minimum; that is why conformance runs use a tight criterion (FIT-CNV-04) and why the trace is always returned.

### Tolerance note: why 1e-4 for fit parameters and 1e-6 for NCA (task T-029)

(No rule; the contract is `AGENTS.md` section 5.) NCA is closed arithmetic on the data: trapezoids, a regression on a chosen window, a few divisions. PKNCA and Caladrius run the same finite sequence of operations, so they differ by rounding and by nothing else, and 1e-6 relative leaves room for the digits PKNCA's own rounding drops. A fit is a search. Three things make two correct fits differ by more than rounding, which is why the contract allows 1e-4 relative on parameters and keeps 1e-6 for the weighted sum of squares:

1. **Stopping rule.** `nlsLM` stops at `ftol = ptol = 1e-15`; the engine stops when the relative decrease of WRSS is below ε (1e-10 in the tests). Near a minimum WRSS is flat, so a parameter error δ costs only about δ² in WRSS, and a rule on the decrease stops with δ of order √ε: 1e-5 for ε = 1e-10 (and for the user default ε = 1e-4 the engine only guarantees what example F1 shows, FIT-CNV-04).
2. **Partial derivatives.** The user default is forward differences with h = 0.001 (FIT-JAC-01). The iteration then stops where the approximated gradient vanishes, not the true one. The oracle uses analytic derivatives, and so do the tests. With the default derivatives the estimates differ from the exact minimum by up to 7e-4 on Indometh (`specs/differences.md` D-03).
3. **Reweighted schemes.** For `inv_yhat` and `inv_yhat2` the target is a fixed point, not a minimum, and WRSS is not flat there (OF-10).

**Measured, not assumed.** `cargo test -p caladrius-fit --test oracle_fit_tight -- --ignored --nocapture` compares the engine (analytic derivatives, ε = 1e-10) with the reference on the 95 fits of the older models and prints the worst gap per quantity: **7.4e-6** on an estimate (`fit_indometh_inv_y`, subject 2, k), **9.2e-6** on a standard error (`fit_theoph_inv_y`, subject 9, ka), **3.8e-7** on WRSS (`fit_indometh_inv_yhat2`, subject 4). The tolerance on parameters is therefore 13 times the worst gap observed: a margin of caution against a different optimizer, not a cushion that was needed to make tests pass; no test was ever relaxed to get there (the one WRSS case that was too coarse was fixed by converging tighter, FIT-CNV-05, OF-10).

**One case checked by hand.** The five-point example of section 11 (F1 to F2: 5 observations, 2 parameters, uniform weights; JᵀJ and Jᵀr are 2 × 2) can be done on paper. Its Gauss-Newton iterates are reproduced to 15 digits by an independent recurrence (`gauss_newton_f1.csv`) and converge to the `nlsLM` minimum; there the engine and R agree to 9.8e-9 on the estimates and 1.3e-8 on the standard errors, and the test `oracle_fit_tight.rs` holds every parameter, statistic, interval limit and secondary parameter of that fit to **1e-6** (`Tolerance::FIT_PARAMETERS_HAND_CHECKED`, stricter than the contract and never used to replace it).
