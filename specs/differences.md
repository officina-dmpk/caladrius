# Documented differences

Places where Caladrius deliberately gives another answer than an oracle value (PKNCA, or later the reference software), or where two methods cannot reach the same number. A gap listed here is a decision, not a failure: it is never absorbed by widening a tolerance (`AGENTS.md` section 5).

## How a difference is recorded

1. An entry below, with an identifier `D-<number>`: what the oracle does, what Caladrius does, why, and which cases and parameters are affected.
2. In the options file of each affected oracle case (`oracle/expected/<case>.options.json`), a `documented_differences` array. Each element has `id` (the identifier above), `parameters` (PKNCA names), optionally `subjects` (a list of subject codes; absent means every subject of the case) and optionally a one-line `note`. The testkit refuses a file whose identifier is malformed or that names a subject or parameter the case does not have.
3. The effect: the testkit comparison (`compare_tables_documented`) leaves those values out and lists them in the report next to the failures; `cargo xtask conformance` counts them in their own column of `docs/conformance.md`, in neither the validated nor the expected totals. A documented value that Caladrius happens to reproduce is still not counted as validated. Moving a row from "validated" to "documented" lowers the validated floor and fails the run, so a difference cannot hide a regression; adding a new one is a deliberate edit of an options file plus an entry here.

Status tags are those of `specs/README.md`. An entry may also record a gap between Caladrius (which agrees with the oracle) and a published formula used as a second source (D-05); it then has no `documented_differences` marker and moves no count.

## D-01 Trailing negative concentration is never Tlast or Clast

- **What PKNCA 0.12.1 does.** With a negative concentration in the data it only warns and goes on. Tlast is the last point whose concentration is not zero, so a negative value at the end of the profile becomes Tlast and its value becomes Clast. The area to Tlast then runs up to that point, and every quantity built on Tlast, Clast or the area to Tlast follows: the extrapolation to infinity, the percentages, clearance, volume and mean residence time. (The terminal-phase regression still uses positive points only. A negative value inside the profile does not move Tlast, and there PKNCA and Caladrius agree on every parameter.) PKNCA's lin-up/log-down areas are not even numbers when a negative value is present, which is why the oracle case uses the linear rule.
- **What Caladrius does.** Under the policy `allow` (NCA-DAT-04) a negative concentration enters the areas by the linear rule, but Tlast and Clast are the last point with a concentration above zero, wherever that is: a negative value is a measurement artefact below the quantification limit, not the last quantifiable observation. For a profile that ends with a negative value this gives an earlier Tlast, a positive Clast, an area to Tlast that stops there (AUCall still reaches the last sample), and the dependent values below.
- **Why.** Clast is the base of the extrapolation `Clast/λz`; a negative Clast would give a negative extrapolated area, a negative percentage and negative volumes or an undefined logarithm in the predicted value. Taking the last positive point keeps every derived parameter meaningful. The choice follows NCA-DAT-04 and the definition of Tlast in NCA-OBS-03 (concentration above zero), and the default policy refuses negative values altogether (`NegativeConcentration`), so this only matters when a user opts into `allow`.
- **Status.** `assumed` for the Caladrius side (NCA-DAT-04 is itself `assumed`); the PKNCA side is `confirmed by oracle` by case `edge_negative_linear`.
- **Affected.** Case `edge_negative_linear`, subject 2 (the profile that ends with -0.2 at 24 h), 16 parameters: `tlast`, `clast.obs`, `clast.pred`, `auclast`, `aumclast`, `aucinf.obs`, `aucinf.pred`, `aumcinf.obs`, `aumcinf.pred`, `aucpext.obs`, `aucpext.pred`, `aumcpext.obs`, `aumcpext.pred`, `cl.obs`, `mrt.obs`, `vz.obs`. Subject 1 (a negative value inside the profile) is not affected and stays validated. Order of magnitude on the case: Tlast 24 h against 12 h, Clast -0.2 against 0.6, AUClast 32.1 against 29.7.
- **Cross-check.** The independent relations between parameters (`crates/caladrius-testkit/tests/edge_consistency.rs`) hold on the PKNCA values, so the oracle is internally sound; the difference is the definition of Tlast, not an arithmetic error on either side.

## D-02 Predicted-value weights on Indometh: the oracle's fixed points are degenerate or missing

- **What the oracle does.** For `inv_yhat` and `inv_yhat2` the target is the iteratively reweighted fixed point of FIT-WGT-03: θ with J(θ)ᵀ·W(θ)·r(θ) = 0, W from the predictions at θ. `oracle/scripts/fit_wls.R` first iterates "refit with frozen weights, then reweight" 100 times; if that has not settled it runs `minpack.lm::nls.lm` on the stationarity equations divided by √(Σ w·y²) and accepts a root when that ratio is below 1e-10. The ratio has the weights in its denominator, so it also becomes small when the predictions go to 0 and the weights 1/ŷ explode, without the gradient being 0.
  - `fit_indometh_inv_yhat`, subjects 1, 3, 5, 6 (`solved_by: root_finding`): the recorded estimates are V ≈ 0.003 to 0.005 and k ≈ 27.6 to 28.1 (C0 of several thousand mg/L, predictions of 1e-90 at 8 h). There the weighted sum of squares is about 1e89 to 1e91 and the stationarity equations, divided by √(Σ y²) instead, are 15 to 26: not a fixed point.
  - `fit_indometh_inv_yhat2`, subjects 1, 3, 4, 5, 6 are listed as `no_fixed_point` (the refit iteration cycles and the root search failed from where it stopped).
- **What Caladrius does.** Its Gauss-Newton iteration recomputes the weights at every iteration (FIT-WGT-03) and stops by FIT-CNV-01. On these subjects it converges to ordinary fixed points: `inv_yhat` subject 1 V = 19.9291, k = 0.641359 (WRSS 0.7171), subject 3 V = 11.0798, k = 0.698727, subject 5 V = 16.8873, k = 0.684850, subject 6 V = 13.2584, k = 0.593548, with the stationarity equations at 1e-10 to 1e-13 relative to √(Σ w·y²); `inv_yhat2` subject 1 V = 28.3650, k = 0.421017, subject 3 V = 16.3903, k = 0.447710, subject 4 V = 19.5880, k = 0.439206, subject 5 V = 25.8137, k = 0.409829, subject 6 V = 18.0510, k = 0.408853, stationary to 1e-8 or better (rounding level of the 1/ŷ² weights). On the subjects where the oracle found a genuine fixed point (inv_yhat 2 and 4, inv_yhat2 2) both agree to 1e-9.
- **Why.** A fixed point is defined by the stationarity equations; the points above satisfy them, the oracle's do not (or the oracle reports none). The engine is not changed to reproduce a degenerate answer.
- **Status.** `assumed` until the oracle is regenerated with an unnormalised (or prediction-independent) stationarity test; the engine side is checked by `crates/caladrius-fit/src/tests.rs` (`predicted_value_weights_reach_a_fixed_point`, independent evaluation of Jᵀ·W·r) and was probed on these subjects (task T-011a).
- **Affected.** `fit_indometh_inv_yhat` subjects 1, 3, 5, 6, every parameter of the case (estimates, WRSS, statistics, intervals, information criteria, secondary), and the `no_fixed_point` list of `fit_indometh_inv_yhat2` (subjects 1, 3, 4, 5, 6: the engine reports convergence). The `documented_differences` markers in the two options files, or a regeneration of the cases, are for the oracle agent; until then six `indometh_inv_yhat::*` tests and `indometh_inv_yhat2::converges` fail.

### D-02 resolution (task T-018): withdrawn, the oracle was wrong

The engine's claims were checked independently and upheld; the oracle (`oracle/scripts/fit_wls.R`) was corrected and `fit_indometh_inv_yhat` and `fit_indometh_inv_yhat2` regenerated. No method difference remains, so D-02 is closed and no case carries a `documented_differences` marker for it.

- **Degenerate "solutions".** For the IV bolus the fixed-point equations of FIT-WGT-03 reduce to sum(r) = 0 and sum(t·r) = 0 with weights 1/ŷ, and to sum(r/ŷ) = 0 and sum(t·r/ŷ) = 0 with weights 1/ŷ². At the recorded points of `inv_yhat` subjects 1, 3, 5, 6 (V near 0.004, k near 28) the first sum was about 15 to 26 times √Σy² (200-bit arithmetic: the terms do not cancel at all); the oracle accepted them because it divided the stationarity equations by √(Σ w·y²), which grows with the weights as fast as the equations shrink. The acceptance test is now the cancellation of the stationarity sums against the sum of the absolute values of their terms (scale-free, independent of the size of the weights), at 1e-6 for a settled iteration and 1e-10 for a root.
- **Missing fixed points.** The plain "refit then reweight" iteration cycles for `inv_yhat2` on these subjects (an unstable fixed point of the iteration map), and the old root search started from where the cycle stopped. A search from a grid of starts finds one root per subject and per scheme (unique over the grid V 1 to 300, k 0.02 to 10): `inv_yhat` 19.9291 / 0.641359, 15.6067 / 0.494665, 11.0798 / 0.698727, 14.0188 / 0.643913, 16.8873 / 0.684850, 13.2584 / 0.593548; `inv_yhat2` 28.3650 / 0.421017, 19.0765 / 0.380325, 16.3903 / 0.447710, 19.5880 / 0.439206, 25.8137 / 0.409829, 18.0510 / 0.408853 (V / k, subjects 1 to 6), equal to the engine's to the digits it printed. In 200-bit arithmetic the stationarity sums cancel to 1e-14 of their terms (1e-9 for the one settled iteration, `inv_yhat2` subject 2).
- **Tolerance note.** At a fixed point of a reweighted scheme WRSS is not stationary in the estimates (d ln WRSS / d ln V is 1 for `inv_yhat` and 2 for `inv_yhat2`, d ln WRSS / d ln k between 1 and 4), unlike the minimum of a fixed-weight fit where WRSS is flat. A relative error of the estimates of 3e-7 therefore appears in WRSS at about 1e-6, the tolerance `FIT_WEIGHTED_SS`. Whether that tolerance should be derived for reweighted schemes is a decision for the orchestrator (AGENTS.md section 5); the oracle was not loosened.

## D-03 Fits with forward-difference derivatives of increment 0.001 do not reach the exact minimum to 1e-4

- **What the oracle does.** The reference fits (R `nlsLM`, `nls`) and their statistics use analytic partial derivatives, and every oracle test of the engine runs with `Derivatives::Analytic` and the tight criterion of FIT-CNV-04 (relative decrease 1e-10).
- **What Caladrius did by default until T-040** (now the preset `reference_conventions`, D-04). Forward differences with a relative increment of 0.001 (FIT-JAC-01, `assumed`, from `AGENTS.md` section 6). The iteration stops where the approximated gradient is zero, which is not where the true gradient is, so the estimates differ from the exact minimum by about the error of the increment times the size of the residuals.
- **Measured (task T-029, 2026-10-09).** All 185 reference fits (95 of the older models, 90 of the T-029 ones) re-run with forward differences and the reference run's convergence settings: 40 of the 490 estimates differ from the reference by more than 1e-4 relative (31 on Indometh, 3 on Theoph, 6 on the lag model). Worst gap per dataset: Indometh 6.9e-4 (`inv_y`, subject 2, k), Theoph 1.4e-4, lag 1.8e-4, infusion 2.3e-5, zero-order 3.3e-5, five-point example 1.2e-5; worst WRSS gap 9.8e-4 (Indometh `inv_yhat`). Standard errors are further off, by 1.2e-3 to 3.5e-3 (FIT-JAC-03).
- **Why it is not a tolerance to widen.** The contract tolerances compare two fits of the same objective computed the same way (AGENTS.md section 5); here the two sides solve slightly different problems (exact Jacobian against a 0.1 % finite difference). How the reference software forms its derivatives is open item OF-01. The engine offers both (`derivatives`), the oracle tests the exact one, and the tolerance note of `specs/fit.md` section 10 gives the margin on the exact one (about 14 times on estimates).
- **Stopping rule (measured by the engine agent in task T-030, reproduced by the reviewer).** The stopping rule matters more than the derivatives. Estimates beyond 1e-4 of the reference, out of the 490 estimates of the 185 reference fits:

  | setting | estimates beyond 1e-4 | worst gap |
  |---|---|---|
  | forward differences 1e-3, reference stopping rule (relative decrease 1e-10) | 40 | 6.9e-4 |
  | forward differences 1e-5, reference stopping rule | 0 | 1.1e-5 |
  | forward differences 1e-3, default stopping rule (relative decrease 1e-4) | 168 | 5.8e-3 |
  | forward differences 1e-5, default stopping rule | 149 | 5.1e-3 |
  | analytic derivatives, default stopping rule | 149 | 5.1e-3 |

  ("Default stopping rule" is the default before T-040, now the preset.) So a fit with those settings (forward differences, convergence 1e-4, 50 iterations) is accurate to about 5e-3 relative; with the tight stop, a finer increment (1e-5) or the exact derivatives bring it within 1e-4. Those settings are the `assumed` reference-software defaults of `AGENTS.md` section 6.
- **Decision.** Settled by Q-014 (`board/QUESTIONS.md`), answered option 2 on 2026-10-09 and applied by task T-040: the default fit uses closed-form derivatives when the model has them (otherwise forward differences of increment 1e-5) and stops at a relative decrease of 1e-10, 50 iterations at most; the former defaults remain the preset `reference_conventions` (D-04). Measured with the default options on all 185 reference fits (`crates/caladrius-fit/tests/default_settings.rs`, a gate): every fit converges, in at most 22 iterations; 0 of 490 estimates beyond 1e-4 (worst 7.4e-6, Indometh `inv_y`, subject 2, k); worst WRSS gap 3.8e-7, worst standard-error gap 9.2e-6. With the preset the same run gives 168 of 490 (worst 5.9e-3, standard errors 9.2e-3), the third row of the table above. v0.1.0 shipped the former defaults.
- **Status.** `assumed` (the preset's values are `assumed`). No oracle case carries a `documented_differences` marker for it, because no test compares a forward-difference fit with the references' estimates at 1e-4; the claim is reproducible with the T-029 oracle files (the forward-difference groups of `crates/caladrius-fit/tests/oracle_fit_models.rs` only check convergence) and by the ignored report of `tests/default_settings.rs`.
- **Open.** Nothing for the default. How the reference software forms its derivatives and which quantity its criterion applies to stay OF-01 and OF-02.

## D-04 The default fit settings differ from the assumed reference defaults, which remain the preset `reference_conventions`

- **What the reference software is assumed to do.** Forward differences with a relative increment of 0.001, convergence 0.0001, 50 iterations (`AGENTS.md` section 6, `assumed`; OF-01, OF-02).
- **What Caladrius does by default (task T-040).** `derivatives = auto`: closed-form derivatives when the model has them for the fitted parameters, otherwise forward differences with h = 1e-5 (FIT-JAC-01); relative decrease of WRSS below 1e-10 (FIT-CNV-01); 50 iterations (FIT-CNV-02). The result records the derivatives used. The assumed reference defaults are one option away: `"preset": "reference_conventions"` in the options of `fit.run` (CLI, MCP, project file), or `FitOptions::reference_conventions()` in Rust; explicit values given with a preset replace its values.
- **Why.** Q-014, option 2 (the human, 2026-10-09): a user who clicks "Fit" gets the exact minimum within the contract tolerance (D-03: 0 of 490 estimates beyond 1e-4 instead of 168), at a cost of a few more iterations (at most 22 on the reference fits). A Caladrius choice, not a claim about the reference software.
- **Status.** `assumed` (a design choice). The default's agreement with the exact minimum is checked on every reference fit by `crates/caladrius-fit/tests/default_settings.rs`; the preset reproduces the trace of the former default on `fit_indometh_inv_y` subject 2 (`crates/caladrius-fit/src/tests_presets.rs`).
- **Affected.** Every fit run without explicit derivative or convergence options: its estimates move by up to 5.9e-3 relative towards the exact minimum and its standard errors by up to 9.2e-3 (measured on the reference fits, D-03). No oracle case: the oracle tests set the reference run's settings explicitly (FIT-CNV-04), and the worked examples F1 to F3 are run with the preset.

## D-05 Steady state with a lag: S-36's printed branch fails where the earlier dose is not yet in its decay phase; Caladrius follows the physical superposition (MOD-MD-05)

- **What the other side does.** The paper S-36 (Bertrand and Mentré 2008, the closed forms of the Monolix library: a second source for equations, not the reference software and not an oracle of the contract) prints the steady-state concentration with a lag as branches of the time s since the dose (first-order input, p. 10 eq. 1.16; zero-order input, p. 14 eq. 1.26). For s at or below the lag, the branch treats the previous dose as being in its decay phase, at the elapsed time s + τ − tlag since the dose. That is valid only when the previous dose is already decaying there: τ ≥ tlag for a first-order input, τ ≥ tlag + T for a zero-order input. S-36 states only τ > T (p. 4), so the printed form is used outside its domain by a user who follows the stated conditions. At τ < tlag + T the previous dose is still being absorbed (or has not started, when tlag ≥ τ) and the printed elapsed time is negative or the wrong phase.
- **What Caladrius does.** The shift rule of MOD-MD-05: the steady-state profile with a lag is the profile without a lag read at s − tlag brought into [0, τ) by whole multiples of τ. It is exact for every τ > 0 and every tlag ≥ 0, because a lag delays the input of every dose by tlag and a periodic profile shifted in time is the same profile in another phase; it is also what the plain sum of shifted single-dose functions gives (MOD-MD-01). For τ ≥ tlag + T (zero-order) or τ ≥ tlag (first-order) it equals S-36's printed form to 1e-14.
- **Why.** The oracle values are plain sums of the single-dose closed forms in 256-bit arithmetic (a tail below 2^-70 of the smallest value), cross-checked within 1e-8 by the periodic solution of the linear system and by trains of dosing events of the ODE; they contain no branch, so no condition on τ. The printed form disagrees in value, in sign and in the shape of the profile: it is not a rounding or a tolerance question. Widening a tolerance to hide a value of −123 against 8.5 would defeat the test (`AGENTS.md` section 5), and the printed form fails exactly where its unstated condition fails, which is why it is recorded here and not absorbed.
- **Example (numeric, from the oracle).** Case `model_md_pk1_oral_0_lag_conv_tau_2p5_tlag_1` (zero-order absorption with T = 2, D = 100, V = 10, CL = 2, tlag = 1, τ = 2.5; tlag + T = 3 exceeds τ): at s = 0 the oracle and Caladrius give C_ss = 20.5207326577 (file value 20.520732657683638), the printed form of S-36 gives 23.1500056096. Second example, first-order absorption `model_md_pk1_oral_1_lag_conv_tlag_gt_tau` (ka = 1, tlag = 8.5, τ = 6): 8.5043435656 against −123.1678150595 (a negative concentration). Third, `model_md_pk1_oral_0_lag_conv_tau_3_tlag_2` (τ = 3, tlag = 2): 16.7766769428 against 22.3117458968. The control `model_md_pk1_oral_0_lag_conv_tau_6_tlag_1` (τ = 6 ≥ tlag + T = 3): 6.4729069939 for both.
- **Status.** `confirmed by oracle` for the Caladrius side (MOD-MD-05, the 28 cases below and every other lagged steady-state case); the printed form is evaluated by the oracle script and stored in the `results_of_checks.s36_printed_form` field of each options file (the branch, `differs_from_oracle`, the times that differ, the printed and the oracle values), but no test compares it with a Caladrius result except `the_engine_follows_the_shift_rule_not_the_printed_branch` in `crates/caladrius-models/tests/oracle_models_md.rs`, which asserts that the engine differs from it by more than 1e-9 relative where it should.
- **Affected.** 28 oracle cases, in `oracle/expected/models/md/` (names without the prefix `model_md_`): in the group `convention` (22) `pk1_oral_0_lag_conv_tau_{2_tlag_1,2_tlag_5,2p5_tlag_1,2p999999_tlag_1,3_tlag_2,T_plus_2e6,T_plus_2e9,eq_T}`, `pk1_oral_1_lag_conv_tlag_{2tau,above_tau_1e6,gt_tau}` and the same eleven for `pk2` (`pk2_oral_0_lag_conv_tau_*`, `pk2_oral_1_lag_conv_tlag_*`); in the group `steady_state` (6) `pk1_oral_0_lag_ss_tau_{10_tlag_12,6_tlag_5p5,6_tlag_6}` and `pk2_oral_0_lag_ss_tau_{12_tlag_11,12_tlag_12,20_tlag_30}`. These are 28 of the 132 steady-state cases; in each the printed form differs at some of the grid times (listed in `times_that_differ` of the options file) and agrees at the others. No Caladrius value differs from an oracle value: the 28 cases are validated, so none carries a `documented_differences` marker and no count of `docs/conformance.md` moves; the difference is with the paper, not with the oracle. Users of S-36's formulas, or of an implementation that copies them, get these values outside τ ≥ tlag + T (zero-order) or τ ≥ tlag (first-order).
- **Open.** Nothing. Whether the reference software uses the physical rule or a branched form is not known to the reader (its documentation is not used for this); a private export with a lag at steady state would decide it (OM-17 (e) in `specs/models.md`).
